Section 01 — Summary
Three converging signals
The transcriptomic profile shows three independent, mechanistically linked abnormalities. Each one is a thread; together they may form the molecular basis of Long COVID persistence.
① Mitochondrial dissociation
Maintenance machinery ↑ · OXPHOS ↓
MTERF1 TWNK RARS2 elevated — the cell is trying to repair and transcribe mtDNA. Yet MT-CO1 MT-ATP6 NDUFA13 and all OXPHOS complexes I–V are silenced. Energy production is decoupled from maintenance effort.
② Complement chronic activation
Soluble regulators ↑ · MAC terminal pathway =
C4BPA CFH C1S overexpressed. Classical pathway initiator activated but terminal MAC (C5b-9) not formed. Pattern: sustained low-grade inflammation without cell lysis — consistent with viral immune evasion.
③ Lymphocyte exhaustion
LAG-3 ↑ across T, NK, immune-reg · ISGs ↓
LAG3 76.8 (ref 17.0–71.8) overexpressed in 3 independent panels. IFNB1 elevated but its downstream ISGs (ISG15, CMPK2) silenced — interferon paradox suggesting viral-driven uncoupling.
🦠 SARS-CoV-2 mechanistic context — for Dr. Jaimes

All three signals are consistent with known SARS-CoV-2 evasion strategies. NSP3 and NSP4 anchor the replication complex to mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs), disrupting mitochondrial dynamics. ORF3a activates the NLRP3 inflammasome while simultaneously suppressing type I IFN signaling — which would explain the IFNB1/ISG15 paradox observed here. Spike protein has been shown to directly interact with ACE2 on mitochondrial membranes, and persistent Spike antigen has been detected months post-infection. The complement data (C4BPA, CFH up; C3AR1 down) matches the "complement dysregulation without membrane attack" pattern described by Afrin et al. 2020 and Jacobs et al. 2022 in long-haulers.

Ref: Jiang H et al., Science 2021 · Jacobs et al., JCI 2022 · Choudhary et al., Cell Reports 2021

Section 02 — Mitochondria
The OXPHOS paradox
The cell is building more mitochondrial maintenance machinery than ever — but the respiratory chain itself is failing. This dissociation is the central metabolic abnormality.
▲ Elevated — mtDNA maintenance & translation
▼ Silenced — respiratory chain OXPHOS
OXPHOS pathway status — by complex
🔬 Mechanistic hypothesis for Dr. Jaimes

The elevation of MTERF1 (mitochondrial transcription terminator factor 1) alongside silenced OXPHOS is a potential smoking gun. MTERF1 normally terminates transcription at the 16S rRNA gene. When overexpressed, it can outcompete TFAM (mitochondrial transcription factor A) for D-loop binding, effectively blocking the transcription of Complex I–IV subunits encoded by mtDNA.

This is exactly the type of dominant negative mtDNA control that SARS-CoV-2 NSP12 (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase) has been proposed to induce by competing with mitochondrial RNA polymerase (POLRMT), diverting polymerase activity away from mitochondrial genome transcription. If viral RNA persists — even at low levels — this competition could sustain the phenotype.

Key question for your expertise: Is NSP12's affinity for POLRMT sufficient to explain persistent MTERF1 upregulation post-acute infection?
Section 03 — Immune Signaling
Interferon paradox & lymphocyte exhaustion
The immune transcriptome shows a pattern consistent with chronic viral antigen persistence: innate signaling initiated but blocked downstream, with adaptive exhaustion across multiple lymphocyte lineages.
IFN type I axis — signal initiated, effectors silenced
Interferon paradox: IFNB1 is elevated in 3 independent panels (Antiviral, Autoimmunity, Cellular Danger) — yet ISG15, CMPK2, IFI6, and TICAM1 are all silenced. This decoupling suggests the IFN signal is being generated but blocked downstream, consistent with SARS-CoV-2 NSP1-mediated translational suppression of ISG mRNAs.
Checkpoint & exhaustion — LAG-3, TIM-3, co-stimulation
LAG-3 upregulation across T cells, NK cells, and immune-regulatory lineages signals chronic exhaustion similar to tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes or chronic viral infections (HBV, HCV, HIV). The simultaneous loss of TIM-3 (HAVCR2↓) and its ligand Galectin-9 (LGALS9↓) may reflect compensatory receptor shedding.
Complement system — chronic activation pattern
C4BPA · CFH · C1S → ▲ soluble regulators overexpressed C3AR1 → ▼ anaphylatoxin receptor silenced MAC C5b-9 → not activated
🦠 SARS-CoV-2 immune evasion — connection to these findings

ORF3a directly suppresses the STING-IRF3 axis, which would explain why IFNB1 is induced (through TLR3/TICAM1-independent pathways) but downstream ISGylation (ISG15) is blocked. NSP1 inserts into the 40S ribosome and suppresses translation of capped mRNAs — ISG mRNAs are particularly vulnerable. The complement pattern (classical pathway initiated by C1S, but negative feedback from C4BPA and CFH preventing terminal activation) matches the "frustrated complement" described in COVID-19 survivors.

The Th17 skewing (RORC↑, IL12B↑, AHR↓) combined with LAG-3 exhaustion represents an immunologically conflicted state — cells want to fight but are simultaneously being suppressed.

Ref: Xia H et al., Cell Host Microbe 2020 (ORF3a) · Thoms M et al., Science 2020 (NSP1) · Gao T et al., medRxiv 2020 (complement)

Q1The hub SDHA (Complex II, elevated) sits between OXPHOS and TCA. Could succinate accumulation from SDHA-SDHC imbalance be activating HIF-1α and sustaining the IFNB1 signal even in the absence of viral replication?
Q2MT-CO1 (Complex IV, most silenced in absolute value) has known structural sites for Cu²⁺ coordination. Is there any evidence that SARS-CoV-2 proteins perturb copper homeostasis in mitochondria, which would affect cytochrome c oxidase assembly?
Q3The MTERF1–TWNK–TEFM triad (all elevated) forms the mtDNA maintenance complex. Could this be a cellular stress response to viral RNA competing with mitochondrial RNAP — and is this reversible?
Q4LAG-3 binds MHC-II directly. Given that HLA-DRB1 and HLA-DQB1 are both silenced, is antigen presentation compromised enough to prevent clearance of residual antigen — creating a self-sustaining exhaustion loop?

Section 06 — Proposed Targets
Priority proteins for structural analysis
Based on network centrality, biological context, and SARS-CoV-2 literature, these are the proteins where your structural expertise would be most impactful.
🔴 Priority 1 — Mitochondrial core
SDHA MTERF1 MT-CO1 RARS2 TWNK MT-ATP6 COQ2 ALAS2
Why: SDHA is the hub connecting OXPHOS II to TCA. MTERF1 is the proposed dominant negative for OXPHOS transcription. MT-CO1 has the largest absolute deficit. AlphaFold2 structures available for all.
🟢 Priority 2 — Immune checkpoint
LAG3 IFNB1 HAVCR2 HLA-DRB1 ISG15 TICAM1 RORC
Why: LAG-3 overexpression in T+NK+Immune-Reg simultaneously is unusual — not typical of a single disease. The LAG-3/MHC-II interaction could be a therapeutic target if antigen persistence drives exhaustion.
🟡 Priority 3 — Complement & evasion
C4BPA CFH C1S C3AR1 NLRP3 AHR HMOX1
Why: C4BPA (log₂FC +2.0) is the most elevated gene in the dataset. Its structural interaction with Protein S (complement inhibitor) may be relevant to the thrombotic/vascular phenotype reported in Long COVID.
